## Technical editorial findings (actionable)

1. **Authentication and authorization remain separate:** an EMV 3DS authentication result may be followed by issuer decline. Avoid describing frictionless 3DS as “3DS skipped” or treating liability shift as automatic.
2. **Credential and account must not be collapsed:** FPAN identifies the funding card credential; a DPAN commonly denotes a device token; a merchant network token and a proprietary PSP vault token have distinct domains and portability constraints. Token existence is neither account funding nor payment approval.
3. **A payment API's success response is not financial success:** an HTTP 2xx response, `pending` status, consent signature or agentic mandate does not prove authorization, capture, merchant order creation or bank payout. All must be reconciled through authoritative events.
4. **PCI architecture changes questions, not automatic scope:** distinguish redirect, iframe/embedded provider page, direct post, and merchant-controlled scripts; verify applicability and all PCI SSC eligibility requirements rather than relying on a single FAQ.
5. **Regulatory examples need jurisdiction and temporal scope:** a Singapore-specific consumer protection workflow must not be copied into other markets or described as covering unrelated scams.
6. **Financial controls need double-entry quality:** keep separate immutable source events for auth, capture, settlement, refund, dispute, reserve and payout; only recognized contractual/finance-approved events belong in gross-to-net reconciliation.
7. **Agentic authorizations are bounded decisions:** validate merchant, line items, amount/currency, substitution, timing and revocation. A signed intent helps establish what was authorized but does not settle fulfillment disputes or guarantee an issuer approval.
